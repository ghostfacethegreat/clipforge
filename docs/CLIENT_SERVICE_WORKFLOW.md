# ClipForge Client Service Workflow

> A practical operating guide for offering short-form editing as a small service.
> This is a workflow guide, not a promise of client demand, campaign acceptance, views, or income.

Related engineering task: [Issue #1 — client-ready clipping workflow](https://github.com/ghostfacethegreat/clipforge/issues/1).

## The first offer

Start with one narrow service: turn a client's owned or properly authorized long-form video into a small batch of reviewed vertical clips.

A simple pilot can be five finished clips. A recurring package can specify a fixed number of clips each month. Set the exact price only after timing a sample job and accounting for review, revisions, storage, and delivery. Do not promise viral reach, view counts, follower growth, or content-rewards payouts.

### Define the scope before accepting work

Every quote should state:

- Number of clips and maximum source-video length.
- Target platforms and aspect ratio.
- Caption language, branding, and style.
- Whether titles/descriptions are included.
- Delivery format and deadline.
- Number of included revision rounds.
- What counts as an out-of-scope change.
- Who owns and retains the source files and final edits.
- Payment method, amount, due date, and refund/cancellation terms agreed with the client.

A quote is not a contract until the client and service provider agree to the terms. Do not let an AI agent accept or alter terms on the owner's behalf.

## Source-media rights checklist

Before editing, record the source URL or uploaded file name and obtain a client confirmation that they:

- Own the source media, or have permission/license rights to edit it.
- Have permission to use any third-party music, footage, images, logos, or appearances in the intended clips.
- Are authorized to request publication to the selected account, if publication is part of the separate scope.
- Understand that permission to edit does not necessarily override the source platform's terms or give rights to music/third-party material.

Prefer original files supplied by the rights-holder. A publicly viewable video is not automatically public domain or reusable. Do not download or redistribute content just because a URL is accessible. Do not process a creator's video, livestream, or streamer's content without appropriate authorization.

Keep the evidence reference (for example, an email confirmation date or a client-signed permission note) with the project. Do not collect identity documents or social passwords.

## Production workflow

1. **Intake:** create a project folder and record client, scope, source, rights confirmation, deadline, and delivery destination.
2. **Source check:** verify the file plays, the audio is usable, and the requested duration/platform is supported.
3. **Create candidates:** use local transcription and moment ranking. Treat model-generated timestamps and scores as suggestions, not facts.
4. **Edit:** trim, reframe, caption, and export using the agreed settings.
5. **Human review:** watch every exported clip from beginning to end; check captions, audio sync, framing, names, context, and accidental private information.
6. **Client preview:** share only the agreed preview files through a private, client-approved method.
7. **Revisions:** apply only the included revision rounds and log additional work before proceeding.
8. **Delivery:** provide final files plus a manifest; do not publish or submit clips to rewards programs unless separately authorized.
9. **Closeout:** ask the client to confirm receipt, record the invoice/receipt status, remove source assets according to the retention agreement, and prepare a renewal draft if appropriate.

## Delivery manifest

For each clip, record:

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Clip filename | clip-001.mp4 |
| Duration | 00:34 |
| Aspect ratio | 9:16 |
| Resolution | 1080x1920 |
| Captions | Reviewed |
| Audio | Reviewed |
| Rights/source | Client-authorized source reference |
| Client status | Preview / approved / delivered |
| Published | No, unless separately authorized |

Never label a clip "published" just because it was rendered, uploaded as a preview, or submitted to a campaign.

## Payment and recordkeeping

Agree on payment method before beginning work. A local customer may agree to pay cash in person; provide a dated receipt and record the gross amount, date, and project. For remote work, use only a payment method the provider permits for the account holder. Do not bypass identity verification, use another person's financial account without authorization, or store card/bank details in ClipForge.

Record quotes, invoices, cash received, refunds, and expenses separately. A quotation or unpaid invoice is not cash received. Keep records for tax purposes; cash earnings are not automatically tax-free.

## Example customer message

> Hi — I make short-form clips from videos that the creator owns or has authorized for editing. I can prepare a small five-clip pilot with captions and vertical framing so you can review the style before deciding on ongoing work. I won't publish anything or use your footage outside the agreed project. Would a sample edit from a video you have permission to share be useful?

Personalize this message for one relevant prospect. Do not send it in bulk or represent that the prospect has already agreed.

## Simple economics

Track time for intake, editing, review, revisions, delivery, and admin. A minimum sustainable quote should account for all of that time, not just the minutes spent rendering.

Use:

**Quote = estimated total hours × your target hourly rate + direct costs + revision/risk allowance.**

After each pilot, compare quoted hours with actual hours. Raise or narrow the scope when actual work consistently exceeds the estimate. Do not confuse estimated campaign CPMs, predicted views, or a platform dashboard estimate with money paid to you.

## What ClipForge must never do by default

- Publish, upload, or submit media automatically.
- Treat accessible URLs as proof of permission.
- Store customer social passwords or payment credentials.
- Fabricate client approvals, campaign submissions, views, or earnings.
- Make claims that clips are guaranteed to go viral or earn rewards.
- Upload client source media to a hosted AI service without explicit, informed approval.

The default is local editing, private preview, human review, and client-controlled publication.
