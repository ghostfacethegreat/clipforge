# Income Engine Playbook

This guide consolidates the owner's existing money-making tracks into one evidence-based pipeline. It is an operating plan, not a promise of employment, customers, or income.

## Operating rule

Run no more than two active sales experiments at a time:

1. **Primary:** one small fixed-scope service that can be delivered with current tools.
2. **Secondary:** one skill-specific commission or job search (currently Roblox/Luau).

Keep software product sales and content-reward campaigns in a separate validation queue until delivery, rights, and payout paths are verified.

Every opportunity must have a public source, an exact requested outcome, a budget or an explicit "not stated", a deadline, eligibility/payment requirements, and one next action. "Interesting creator" is not a qualified lead. A quote is not revenue. A view count is not earnings. Only money actually received is cash received.

## Current service menu

### 1. Creator Shorts editing — primary experiment

**Introductory pilot:** 3 vertical clips for $25, from one source video up to 30 minutes, each clip up to 60 seconds, captions, basic audio cleanup, and one reasonable revision round. Turnaround target: 3 days after receiving a usable source, written permission, and agreed payment/deposit.

**Only accept:** footage supplied by the client or media they can authorize for the intended editing and use. Confirm permission for third-party music, images, logos, appearances, and intended platform use. Do not scrape public videos or assume a creator's campaign brief licenses all underlying game/music rights.

**Delivery standard:** three MP4s, reviewed end-to-end, a manifest with filenames/duration/aspect ratio/caption/audio status, and a private preview. Posting, account access, campaign submissions, and social-media management are excluded.

**Evidence needed before selling widely:** create one sample using footage the owner created or has explicit permission to edit; inspect captions, pacing, audio, crop, and export quality on a phone-size screen.

Related: [ClipForge client workflow](CLIENT_SERVICE_WORKFLOW.md), [editing offer](CLIP_EDITING_OFFER.md).

### 2. Roblox / Luau scripting — secondary experiment

Offer small, bounded tasks rather than claiming to be a full-service studio:

- One reproducible bug fix.
- One isolated gameplay mechanic.
- One small UI or inventory/shop feature.
- One performance review with a short findings report.

Before quoting, ask for the exact expected behaviour, current code/sample place file, target devices, data persistence requirements, acceptance test, deadline, and whether the buyer needs a fixed price or milestone.

Keep client/server authority on the server for currency, rewards, purchases, and progression. Never accept requests to steal assets, copy paid systems, bypass moderation, exploit games, or manipulate engagement.

**Evidence needed:** publish one clean demonstration place or redacted code sample with screenshots/video, a README, and a short test checklist. Do not claim years of experience or production systems you have not built.

### 3. Python / AI automation

Start with one narrow workflow the buyer can describe in a sentence:

- Rename/organize files with a dry-run preview.
- Convert CSV/JSON formats and generate a report.
- Automate a repeated spreadsheet or document task.
- Add a small API integration where the buyer supplies authorized credentials through a secure agreed process.

Deliver source code, a setup README, a sample input/output, tests, and a one-page handover. Do not ask clients to paste secrets into chat or commit \`.env\` files. Prefer local/offline solutions when possible.

### 4. Simple business websites

Start with a small static site or site refresh, not an unlimited custom web application:

- One landing page with services, opening hours, contact links, and a map/directions link.
- Mobile responsive layout and accessibility basics.
- Owner-approved copy/images.
- Deployment and handover notes, with hosting/domain fees stated separately.

Do not promise search ranking, customer volume, or sales. Quote only after agreeing on pages, content, revisions, hosting ownership, and maintenance.

### 5. DevCut / ClipForge product sales

DevCut's README describes a $30 customer-edition concept and a free owner edition, but fulfillment/payment automation is not live. Before advertising the product as purchasable, verify that the downloadable package actually exists, installs cleanly, has reproducible checksums, and is licensed correctly; test customer delivery and support.

ClipForge is currently more credible as a client-delivery tool than as a polished SaaS product: it runs locally and needs customer-controlled OAuth setup for social publishing. Avoid selling auto-posting capabilities that the buyer cannot actually configure.

### 6. AETHER product commercialization

AETHER is an alpha local-first CLI. The public repository currently declares MIT. Do not advertise the existing MIT core as pay-to-use or add a hidden owner bypass. A separate paid hosted service, support, training, or genuinely separate extensions could be explored after a contributor/third-party rights review and qualified licensing advice.

Track this as product validation, not immediate income. See AETHER issue #19 for the rights-aware commercialization plan.

### 7. GTA/content-reward clipping

**Validation hold:** current campaign scouting has surfaced rates and listings, but commercial rights for source gameplay, stream footage, and music are not consistently verified. Do not download, edit, publish, or submit clips until the source and campaign permissions cover the actual commercial use. Record researched campaigns separately from authorized footage, published clips, approved clips, and payouts.

## Lead qualification template

For each lead, capture:

- Buyer or company name and the public listing URL.
- The exact request and the evidence it is currently open.
- Budget / rate, or "not stated".
- Scope, expected samples, experience requirements, and deadline.
- Age/work eligibility and payout identity requirements, when stated.
- Whether the source media/code is authorized for the proposed work.
- One next step and its due date.

Reject upfront-fee jobs, paid application boosts, fake-check arrangements, reshipping, account/password requests, fake engagement, vague revenue-share-only work, and requests to move money for a stranger.

## Payments before work

Do not start a paid job until the client agrees in writing on scope, price, deposit/milestones, deadline, revision count, cancellation terms, and payment method.

Confirm that the payment provider permits the account holder to receive the funds and that identity/age/payout requirements can actually be met. Do not borrow another person's account, evade KYC, or ask a customer to share their bank or card details in chat. For any lawful local cash arrangement, give a receipt and keep a record. Keep tax records for all income, including cash.

## Weekly operating cycle

- **Scout:** review new leads from the general skills scout and campaign scout.
- **Qualify:** keep only direct, live, relevant listings with a verifiable request and a workable payout route.
- **Proof:** create or improve one portfolio sample that matches the chosen service.
- **Contact:** send a small number of personalized messages or applications manually after reviewing each one.
- **Deliver:** complete one scoped task, test it, get client acceptance, and deliver.
- **Reconcile:** record only money actually received; track expenses separately.
- **Improve:** compare estimated vs actual delivery time, revisions, and net cashflow.
